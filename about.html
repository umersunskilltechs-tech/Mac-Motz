About

Mac Motz

Mac Motz is a documentary and fine-art photographer working in long-form series about the American landscape — the highways, floodplains, and small towns where cultivated land meets the wild edges people try to ignore.

Raised outside a farming county in the Midwest, Mac's first camera was a hand-me-down Pentax K1000 and a summer with nothing else to do. What started as a way to document a disappearing way of life became a decade-long practice: six major series shot between 2015 and the present, each returned to over months or years rather than finished in a single trip.

"I'm not trying to make the ordinary look extraordinary. I'm trying to slow down long enough to notice it was already extraordinary."

Approach

Mac shoots almost entirely on medium-format film, returning to the same locations across seasons to let a place reveal itself rather than composing a single decisive frame. Portraits are made only with the subject's participation — nothing is taken without a conversation first.

Exhibitions & recognition

Work from Low Country and Vacancy has shown in regional gallery group exhibitions across the Midwest and South, and individual plates are held in several private collections. Mac is not currently represented by a gallery and handles print and licensing inquiries directly.
